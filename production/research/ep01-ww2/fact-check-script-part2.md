# Fact-check — ep01-ww2 script, PART 2 (s07–s11)

- **Target:** `production/scripts/ep01-ww2/script.json` (`text` fields), sections s07–s11 (330–600 s)
- **Checked by:** researcher
- **Date:** 2026-11-15
- **Scope note:** s01–s06 are being checked separately (part 1). Pronunciation spellings (`say`) not checked.
- **Method note:** Several sites (britannica.com, iwm.org.uk article pages) returned HTTP 403 to direct fetching. For those, I relied on search-engine snippets of the named pages. All other references are from search snippets of the pages listed under Sources. One site (ddaymuseum.co.uk Q&A URL) returned unrelated spam content and was **not** used.

## Overall verdict

Section s07–s11 is accurate overall. There are no errors of date or name. Two items need a wording change before the script is locked:

1. **s08 S3 (CORRECTION NEEDED, minor):** the ~156,000 figure counts all Allied troops put ashore by sea *and* by air. About 23,000 of them came by parachute and glider, so they did not land "on five beaches".
2. **s11 S1 (DISPUTED):** the total death toll of "70 to 85 million" sits at the top of the range. IWM, the UN, the National WWII Museum and Britannica give about 55–60 million, and Britannica's range is 35–60 million.

Three further NUANCE points are optional refinements.

## Recommended changes

1. **s08 S3: CORRECTION NEEDED (minor; required)**
   - Current: "In the largest seaborne invasion in history, around 156,000 American, British, Canadian and other Allied troops landed on five beaches in Normandy."
   - Replace with: **"In the largest seaborne invasion in history, around 156,000 American, British, Canadian and other Allied troops landed in Normandy, from the sea on five beaches and from the air."**
   - Shorter alternative (if timing is tight): **"In the largest seaborne invasion in history, around 156,000 American, British, Canadian and other Allied troops landed in Normandy, by sea and by air."**
   - Reason: about 23,000 of the ~156,000 were airborne troops (IWM; National WWII Museum).

2. **s11 S1: DISPUTED (recommended)**
   - Current: "The war killed an estimated 70 to 85 million people, most of them civilians."
   - Replace with: **"The war killed an estimated 60 million people or more, most of them civilians."**
   - Alternative that keeps the higher figures: **"The war killed an estimated 60 to 85 million people, most of them civilians."** IWM/UN/National WWII Museum support the 60 million floor; the 85 million ceiling comes only from compilations that add famine and disease deaths, so it is the weaker end.
   - The first option is the safer one. Check consistency with s01 ("Tens of millions of people would die"), which is fine either way.

3. **s10 S6: NUANCE (optional)**
   - Current: "On 8 August, the Soviet Union declared war on Japan and invaded Manchuria."
   - Optional: **"On 8 August, the Soviet Union declared war on Japan, and within hours its armies poured into Manchuria."** S7 ("The next day, a second atomic bomb devastated Nagasaki") then still reads correctly.

4. **s07 S2: NUANCE (optional)**
   - Optional: "...four Japanese aircraft carriers, **a blow from which Japan's navy never fully recovered**." This is slightly more defensible, but the current wording is acceptable.

## Disputed / estimate notes

- **Total war dead (s11):** estimates range from 35–60 million (Britannica) through ~55 million (USHMM) and ~60 million (IWM, UN, National WWII Museum) up to 70–85 million in compilations that include famine and disease deaths, especially in China and the USSR. The National WWII Museum notes that "the number of civilian deaths in China alone might well be more than 50,000,000", which shows how uncertain the upper end is. Always say "estimated".
- **Holocaust:** six million is the standard figure (USHMM, Yad Vashem). Yad Vashem notes scholarly estimates of roughly 5.3–6 million. "Around six million" is correct.
- **Okinawa civilians:** estimates run from ~24,000–42,000 (some US sources) to ~100,000–150,000 (Britannica, National WWII Museum). The script's "well over 100,000 soldiers and civilians" holds under every estimate.
- **Atomic-bomb deaths:** figures vary by method and cut-off date. RERF states "the total number of deaths is not known precisely".
- **Bombs vs Soviet entry:** a live historiographical debate (Hasegawa vs Frank and others). The script correctly presents it as a debate.
- **Midway "never recovered":** Parshall and Tully (*Shattered Sword*) and others revise the old story of the "loss of irreplaceable pilots". The loss of carriers, aircraft and experienced crews, and of the initiative at sea, is not disputed.
- **German surrender dates:** 7 May (Reims) and 8 May (Berlin). Russia marks 9 May. The script's "Days later... on 8 May... VE Day" is correct.
