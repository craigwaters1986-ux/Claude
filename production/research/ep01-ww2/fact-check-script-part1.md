## Recommended changes (exact replacement text)

None of these is a hard error. They are listed in order of priority.

1. **s05 (#54) — sequencing/causation.** Replace
   "So Germany bombed British cities instead. More than 40,000 civilians were killed in the Blitz."
   with
   "From September, Germany turned to bombing British cities. More than 40,000 civilians were killed in the Blitz."
   (The Blitz began on 7 Sept 1940, during the Battle of Britain, not after it. Similar syllable count.)
2. **s03 (#28) — clarity.** Replace "In 1936, German troops marched into the Rhineland." with
   "In 1936, German troops reoccupied the demilitarised Rhineland."
3. **s04 (#35) — precision.** Replace "A secret protocol divided Eastern Europe between them." with
   "A secret protocol divided much of Eastern Europe into German and Soviet spheres of influence."
   (Shorter alternative: "A secret protocol carved up Eastern Europe between them." This is still broad, but reads as a plan rather than an accomplished fact.)
4. **s01 (#2) — overstatement.** Replace "Across Europe, life goes on as normal." with
   "Across much of Europe, life seems to go on as normal."
5. **s06 (#62) — optional.** "Meanwhile, Japan was expanding across Asia and the Pacific." could become "Meanwhile, Japan was expanding across East and South-East Asia."
6. **Storyboard/animation notes (no script change):** at Dunkirk (#49), do not show the little ships carrying most of the army, because most troops left from the mole on destroyers and larger ships. At Munich (#30), Mussolini/Italy was also a signatory if leaders are shown. At Pearl Harbor (#63), the attack also hit Oahu's airfields.

Kept as written although simplified (acceptable): #5 "army was beaten", #12 "ordered to pay huge reparations", #15 "Wall Street Crash triggered the Great Depression", #24 "turned Germany into a dictatorship".

## Disputed / estimate notes

- **WWII total deaths (#4):** estimates vary widely by method (whether famine, disease and the Holocaust are included). Common ranges run from c. 40–60 million in older reference works to c. 70–85 million in recent syntheses. "Tens of millions" is safe. Do not put a precise number on screen.
- **Blitz deaths (#55):** IWM gives "over 43,500"; London Museum "around 43,000"; Hutchinson "about 42,000"; some summaries give 40,000–43,000. The **National WWII Museum "The Blitz of 1940" article gives "over 23,000"**, which is an outlier against UK sources (it is possibly an error or a narrower count). "More than 40,000" stays below every UK figure and is safe.
- **Barbarossa strength (#58):** estimates run from c. 3 million to 3.8 million+ Axis personnel (the NWWII Museum gives c. 3.5 m German plus c. 0.7 m allied); some sources give larger totals including reserves. "More than three million" is safe. Avoid an exact figure on screen.
- **Barbarossa front length (#59):** Britannica gives 2,900 km (1,800 mi); one outlier (SparkNotes) gives ~1,000 miles. "Nearly 3,000 km" matches the authoritative figure.
- **Dunkirk nationality split (#48):** the total of 338,226 is consistent across sources. The British/French split varies (English Heritage: 224,320 British). Do not quote a split.
- **German unemployment (#16):** official registered figure c. 6.1 m (Feb 1932); the true number including unregistered was higher. The rate is given as c. 24% (Dimsdale) to c. 30% (some textbooks). Avoid quoting a percentage.
- **Start of WWII (#39, #43):** some historians (notably in China) date the war from 1931 or 1937. The script's "The Second World War **in Europe** had begun" handles this well. Keep "in Europe".
- **Nazi–Soviet Pact date (#34):** signed in the early hours of 24 Aug 1939 but dated 23 Aug. "August 1939" is safe.
- **Moscow counter-offensive (#61):** usually dated 5–6 Dec 1941 (one source gives 7 Dec). The closest German approach is about 19 km (12 mi) from central Moscow. "Outskirts" is fine.
