Researcher · 2026-11-16 · Lean mode: **no separate post-script fact-check**, so these notes are the
only accuracy gate. Write ONLY from rows marked HIGH or MEDIUM, using the "Safe wording" given.
Source keys (S#) → `sources.md`. "Fetched" = page read directly; "snippet" = read via search summary
(IWM, Britannica, 1914-1918-online blocked fetching). British English; dates as "28 June 1914".

**Golden rules for the script**
1. Every casualty figure is an **estimate** — say "about", "around", "an estimated", or give a range. On-screen: add "(est.)".
2. Causes ≠ triggers. Sarajevo was the **trigger**; the war came from a chain of decisions on top of long-term tensions. No single cause.
3. Debates are debates (war guilt, generals, the Somme). Present two views, don't rule.
4. The Armenian Genocide: name it as genocide; sombre; non-graphic; give it its own beat (see §Sensitive).
5. No invented dialogue for real people; no quotations except those in §Quotations.

## 1. Chronology (master timeline)

**1914**
- 28 Jun — Archduke Franz Ferdinand (heir to Austria-Hungary) and his wife Sophie shot dead in Sarajevo by Gavrilo Princip, 19, a Bosnian Serb (S1, S34).
- early Jul — Germany promises Austria-Hungary unconditional support (the "blank cheque") (S1; date not given there — say "in early July").
- 23 Jul, 6 p.m. — Austro-Hungarian ultimatum to Serbia, 48-hour limit (S1, S2).
- 25 Jul — Serbia replies, accepting most but not all demands; Austria-Hungary breaks off relations and begins mobilising (S2).
- 28 Jul — Austria-Hungary declares war on Serbia (S1, S2).
- 29 Jul (late) — Russia announces partial mobilisation against Austria-Hungary (S1).
- 30 Jul (c. 5 p.m.) — Russia orders general mobilisation (S1; S2 dates its full mobilisation 31 Jul — use "30–31 July") .
- 1 Aug — Germany declares war on Russia; France mobilises (S2, S57).
- 2 Aug — German ultimatum to Belgium demanding passage (S3: "the day before" 3 Aug).
- 3 Aug — Germany declares war on France (S3, S57).
- 4 Aug (morning) — German troops cross into Belgium (S3, S4). Britain, guarantor of Belgian neutrality under the 1839 Treaty of London, sends an ultimatum; it expires **11 p.m. London time (midnight in Berlin)** and Britain is at war with Germany (S1, S3, S4).
- 6 Aug — Austria-Hungary declares war on Russia (S57). 12 Aug — Britain (and France, 11 or 12 Aug) declare war on Austria-Hungary (S57). 23 Aug — Japan declares war on Germany (S57).
- 21 Aug — Pte John Parr, believed first British soldier killed in action, near Mons (S28).
- 22 Aug — c. 27,000 French soldiers killed in one day in the Battle of the Frontiers (S49; popular-history level → MEDIUM).
- 23 Aug — Battle of Mons, BEF's first major battle; retreat follows (S50).
- 26–30 Aug — Tannenberg: German Eighth Army destroys Russian Second Army in East Prussia (S46; exact dates from general knowledge → say "late August").
- 5/6–12 Sep — First Battle of the Marne: German advance on Paris stopped; Schlieffen-style quick victory fails (S49; start date varies 5 or 6 Sep → say "early September").
- mid-Sep–Oct — "Race to the Sea"; trench line from Belgian coast to Swiss border, c. 700 km / c. 440 miles (S49).
- 29 Oct — Ottoman fleet (German-led) raids Russian Black Sea ports; Russia, Britain, France declare war on the Ottoman Empire in early November (S47).
- 24–25 Dec — Christmas Truce, unofficial and patchy (S8, S9, S10).

**1915**
- 19 Jan — first Zeppelin raid on Britain (Great Yarmouth, King's Lynn) (S32).
- 22 Apr — first large-scale chlorine gas attack, Second Battle of Ypres (S37).
- 24 Apr — mass arrest of Armenian community leaders in Constantinople; date commemorated as start of the Armenian Genocide (S17, S19).
- 25 Apr — Gallipoli landings (British, French, Anzac, also Indian & Newfoundland units) (S11, S12).
- 7 May — Lusitania sunk off southern Ireland; c. 1,198 dead incl. c. 128 Americans (S43).
- May (commonly 23 May) — Italy declares war on Austria-Hungary (S47; on Germany only Aug 1916).
- Sep — first British Brodie helmets delivered; French Adrian helmet issued (S52).
- Oct — Bulgaria joins the Central Powers; Serbia overrun Oct–Nov (S47).
- 19–20 Dec Anzac/Suvla evacuated; 8–9 Jan 1916 Helles evacuated — Gallipoli ends (S11, S12).

**1916**
- 27 Jan — Military Service Act: conscription of single men 18–41; in force 10 Feb; extended to married men May 1916 (S30).
- 21 Feb–18 Dec — Battle of Verdun (S14). German Stahlhelm first used here (S52).
- 31 May–1 Jun — Battle of Jutland (S16).
- 4 Jun — Brusilov Offensive opens (S46).
- 1 Jul–18 Nov — Battle of the Somme; first day worst in British Army history (S5, S6).
- 27 Aug — Romania enters the war on the Allied side (S47; MEDIUM).
- 15 Sep — first tanks in action, Flers–Courcelette (S55).

**1917**
- 16/19 Jan — Zimmermann telegram sent/intercepted (S21, S59).
- 1 Feb — Germany resumes unrestricted U-boat warfare (S21).
- 24 Feb — Britain gives the telegram to President Wilson; published in US press 1 Mar (S21, S59).
- 15 Mar (2 Mar old style) — Tsar Nicholas II abdicates (February Revolution) (S24).
- 6 Apr — USA declares war on Germany (Senate 4 Apr, House 6 Apr) (S21, S59).
- 13 Jun — first daylight Gotha raid on London: 162 killed (S32).
- 31 Jul–10 Nov — Third Ypres / Passchendaele (S25).
- Oct–Nov — Caporetto: Italian front collapses (S46/S47, date general → "autumn 1917").
- 7 Nov (25 Oct old style) — Bolsheviks seize power (October Revolution) (S24).

**1918**
- Jan–Jul — rationing in Britain introduced in stages; national ration books July 1918 (S31).
- 3 Mar — Treaty of Brest-Litovsk: Russia leaves the war (S23).
- 21 Mar — German Spring Offensive (Operation Michael) (S7, S26).
- 18 Jul — French counter-attack on the Marne (S7).
- 8 Aug — Battle of Amiens; start of the Hundred Days (S7).
- 29 Sep — Bulgaria's armistice (in force 30 Sep) (S48). 26–29 Sep — Hindenburg Line broken (S7).
- 30 Oct — Ottoman armistice (Mudros) (S48). 3 Nov — Austria-Hungary's armistice (Villa Giusti) (S48).
- late Oct–early Nov — German naval mutiny at Kiel (S48). 9 Nov — Kaiser's abdication announced; he goes into exile in the Netherlands (S48).
- 11 Nov — Armistice signed c. 5 a.m. in a railway carriage at Compiègne; fighting stops 11 a.m. (S7, S27).

**1919**
- 18 Jan — Paris Peace Conference opens (S22: "January 1919").
- 28 Jun — Treaty of Versailles signed in the Hall of Mirrors — five years to the day after Sarajevo (S56).

## 3. Disputed points (present as debates)
- **D1 War guilt.** Fritz Fischer (*Griff nach der Weltmacht*, 1961) argued Germany bore the main responsibility, pursuing aims of expansion and backing Austria-Hungary to provoke war; Fischer himself said "considerable" responsibility, not sole. Christopher Clark (*The Sleepwalkers*, 2012) stresses shared responsibility — many governments' decisions in a tangled system, none intending a world war. Critics (e.g. Winkler, Bogdanor) say Clark underplays German and Austrian refusal of mediation. Current position: few historians argue *sole* German guilt; many put the main responsibility on Germany and Austria-Hungary, while recognising Russian, French and Serbian decisions mattered. (S45) Safe line: "Historians still argue about who was most to blame."
- **D2 "Lions led by donkeys".** The phrase pre-dates WW1 (Crimean-era uses); its attribution to German general Max Hoffmann comes via Alan Clark's 1961 book *The Donkeys* and is unsubstantiated. Since the 1960s many historians (the "learning curve" school) argue British commanders learned and adapted, culminating in 1918's victories; others maintain that generals wasted lives. Never present the phrase as a real wartime quote. (S44)
- **D3 Was the Somme worth it?** One view: catastrophic losses for a few miles. Another: it relieved Verdun, wore down the German army (German losses heavy, figures disputed), and taught the British army hard lessons. German casualty totals range c. 450,000–600,000+. (S6, S58)
- **D4 Casualty totals.** All national and global totals vary by definition (deaths from disease, missing, post-war deaths; civilian famine; whether flu included). Always give ranges. (S40, S41, S42)
- **D5 Verdun aim.** Falkenhayn's later claim that he intended to "bleed France white" is doubted by many historians as after-the-fact justification. Say "Germany attacked Verdun, which France would defend at any cost." (S14 context)
- **D6 Schlieffen Plan.** Whether a fixed "Schlieffen Plan" existed as later described is debated among historians (Zuber thesis). Safe: "Germany's war plan: knock France out fast through Belgium, then turn east." (general — keep wording generic)
- **D7 Versailles → WW2.** "Too harsh" vs "not harsh enough / not enforced" vs the Depression and Nazi choices as decisive. Keep the bridge light: "the peace left grievances that would be exploited."
- **D8 Zimmermann telegram's weight.** Some argue unrestricted U-boat warfare mattered more than the telegram for US entry. Say both together pushed the US into war. (S59)
- **D9 Pardons for the 306.** Campaigners argued many suffered shell shock; some historians caution against assuming all did. (S36, S54)
- **D10 Christmas football.** Partly attested: informal kickabouts in some sectors; the "big organised match" with a score is not reliably evidenced. (S8, S9, S10)

## 5. Sensitive-content guidance
- **Mass death, wounds, gas, shell shock:** non-graphic — silhouettes, empty helmets, falling poppies, dimming light, an empty chair at home; no blood, no corpses in close-up, no comic SFX. Gas: green-yellow cloud drifting, figures fumbling for masks, cut away.
- **Shell shock:** show as trembling, a thousand-yard stare, a hospital bed — compassion, never comedy.
- **Executions (306):** factual, brief; avoid dramatising a firing squad; can show a pardon document dated 2006.
- **Armenian Genocide:** give it its own 10–15 s beat inside §6. Name it: "the Armenian Genocide". Perpetrator: the Ottoman government of the time (Committee of Union and Progress) — not "the Turks" as a people. Visuals: long line of figures walking into a desert at dusk, an empty doorway, a candle. No bodies. Numbers as a range ("an estimated 600,000 to 1.5 million"). One factual line on denial: "Turkey's government still rejects the term genocide; historians and genocide scholars overwhelmingly use it." Mention it was coined in 1944 with this case in mind (optional bridge to ep01/Holocaust — handle carefully). Assyrian and Greek Christian communities also suffered mass killings — do not overstate; optional and UNVERIFIED numbers, omit numbers.
- **Colonial/empire troops & labour:** factual, respectful; show Indian, African, Caribbean, Chinese figures as agents, not props; mention unequal treatment only with care (e.g. carriers' death rates from disease).
- **Propaganda:** label any recreated poster "Propaganda poster (1915)" etc.; no glorification. Avoid real atrocity imagery.
- **Civilians:** the Poplar school deaths (18 children) — mention as a number, sombre; no images of children harmed.
- **Fictional characters:** if a composite soldier/munitionette/nurse guides the story, label as "illustrative" in description and never give a real name. Real people (Princip, Franz Ferdinand, Wilson, Lenin, Foch, Ludendorff, Haig, Zimmermann, Ellison, Gunther, Parr) carry name tags.

## 7. Engaging, verified human details (pick a few)
- Princip was 19 — too young to be executed; he died in prison of tuberculosis in April 1918, months before the war he triggered ended (S34).
- The archduke's car took a wrong turn and stopped near Princip (S34).
- Britain's ultimatum expired at 11 p.m. London time — midnight in Berlin (S1, S4).
- Christmas 1914: carols across no-man's land, souvenirs swapped, the dead buried together; Rifleman Eve: "not a shot fired all day or night" (S8).
- The first and last British soldiers believed killed — John Parr (21 Aug 1914, perhaps only 17) and George Ellison (11 Nov 1918) — lie facing each other in St Symphorien cemetery near Mons; it is a coincidence (S28).
- Munition workers turned yellow from TNT: the "canaries" (S60).
- 13 June 1917: a bomb on a Poplar school killed 18 children — the "first Blitz" (S32).
- Henry Gunther, killed at 10.59 a.m., one minute before the ceasefire (S27).
- Versailles signed five years to the day after Sarajevo — in the same Hall of Mirrors where the German Empire had been proclaimed in 1871 (S56).
- Passchendaele: about 42,900 British Empire dead there have no known grave (S25).
