## Disputed / inconsistent points
- **D1 Collision time:** British report 11.40 p.m. ship's time; US Senate report "11.46 p.m. ship's time, or 10.13 p.m. New York time". Use "about 11.40 p.m.".
- **D2 Aboard / deaths / survivors:** British inquiry 2,201 aboard, 711 saved (32.3%), 1,490 lost; US inquiry 2,223 aboard, 706 saved, 1,517 lost; Encyclopedia Titanica researchers 2,208 / 712 / 1,496 (snippet only). Early press (LOC) gave lower tolls (1,340). Never present one exact number as fact; say "around 1,500" or give the inquiry range "1,490–1,517".
- **D3 Lifeboat capacity:** 1,178 (British report, Britannica) vs 1,176 (US report). Prefer "about 1,178" / "room for barely half".
- **D4 Carpathia arrival / survivors taken aboard:** 705 (Rostron / Britannica snippet) vs 712 (British report). Arrival c. 3.30–4.05 a.m.
- **D5 Distance from Newfoundland:** 370 vs c. 400 nautical miles depending on source — avoid exact distance in the teaser.
- **Not verified, do not use:** "unsinkable" as an official claim, band playing a named hymn, water temperature, any quotation.

## Suggested hooks
1. "At about 11.40 p.m. on 14 April 1912, on her maiden voyage, Titanic struck an iceberg. Less than three hours later, she was gone." (no superlatives such as "largest ship" — not verified here)
2. "Twenty lifeboats. Room for about 1,178 people. More than 2,200 on board." — the arithmetic of the disaster.
3. "By dawn, the Carpathia had found about 700 survivors. Around 1,500 people never came home."

Do not use real victims' names or likenesses without sourcing; fictional illustrative figures must not be presented as real people. Label death tolls as estimates.
