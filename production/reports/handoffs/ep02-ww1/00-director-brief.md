## Audience and angle
16–55, many with little history. Angle: *how a crisis no one fully controlled became a war no one
could stop, and what it did to the people who lived through it* — ordinary soldiers, nurses,
munitions workers and families alongside the decision-makers. It also sets up Episode 1: the
peace of 1919 is the first chapter of WW2, so end with a light bridge to it.

## Sensitive topics and handling
- Mass death, gas, wounds, shell shock: sombre, non-graphic, no comic SFX.
- The Armenian Genocide (1915–): name it accurately as genocide (scholarly consensus), note it is
  denied by the Turkish state, sombre and non-graphic. Use only if it fits the time; if included, give it respect, not a line in passing.
- Executions for desertion/"cowardice", colonial labour and troops: factual, not sensational.
- Casualty figures are estimates with ranges; say so on screen.
- War-guilt debate (Fischer vs. "sleepwalkers"/shared responsibility): present as a debate.
- Christmas Truce: unofficial and patchy, not universal; the football matches are partly attested.
- Avoid myths: "lions led by donkeys" is debated; Gavrilo Princip's sandwich is a myth.
