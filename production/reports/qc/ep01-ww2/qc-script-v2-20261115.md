## 2. Specific review points requested

- **Tone (Holocaust, s09):** PASS. The tone is sombre and factual and nothing is graphic. "Systematically murdered around six million Jewish men, women and children" uses the right agency ("Nazi Germany and its collaborators") and labels the figure as approximate. Other victim groups are named. Pauses of 0.5–0.7 s are written around the passage. Hitler's death is stated neutrally ("took his own life").
- **Tone (Hiroshima/Nagasaki, s10):** PASS. The passage is restrained and not graphic: "Tens of thousands of people died instantly, and many more in the months that followed". There is no triumphal framing, and 0.5 s / 0.4 s pauses follow each bomb.
- **Civilian deaths:** PASS. The Blitz (40,000+ civilians), Okinawa ("soldiers and civilians"), the bombs and "most of them civilians" (s11) are all stated plainly.
- **Causes vs triggers:** PASS. s02 states outright that "Versailles alone did not cause another war" and lists economic, political, nationalist and antisemitic causes. s03 covers ideology plus the decisions on rearmament, the Rhineland, Austria and Munich, and the failure of appeasement. s04 gives the immediate trigger (the Pact, then Poland) and separates "the Second World War **in Europe**" from Japan's war in Asia since 1931/1937.
- **Germany's vs Japan's surrender:** PASS. They are clearly distinguished. Germany: "surrendered unconditionally", VE Day on 8 May, "But in the Pacific, the war went on". Japan: Hirohito's announcement on 15 August, then the formal signing on 2 September 1945.
- **Debate over Japan's surrender:** PASS. "Historians still debate how much the bombs and the Soviet invasion each shaped that decision" (P2 #36, Hasegawa vs Frank). Moving the Soviet invasion to "within hours" (before Nagasaki) makes the sequence fair to both sides. See S2 for a small clarity tweak.
- **Duration fit:** The section windows fit. My own pace calculation is words ÷ (narration end − section start − lead), using the scriptwriter's measured end times:

| Section | Words | Speech span (s) | Pace incl. pauses | Speed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| s01 | 54 | 21.15 | **153 wpm** (~176 wpm between the 2.7 s of written pauses) | **1.139×** |
| s02 | 130 | 57.33 | 136 | 1.075× |
| s03 | 125 | 57.49 | 131 | **1.154×** |
| s04–s10 | 111–123 | 50–58 | 125–136 | 1.000–1.007× |
| s11 | 67 | 27.56 | 146 | 1.000× |

  **s03 is low risk.** At 131 wpm it is in line with the body of the episode. The high multiplier reflects long, many-syllable words rather than a crowded window. A listening check for Kokoro artefacts is enough (S1).
  **s01 is a real risk of sounding rushed** (#1). It is the fastest section in the episode by a clear margin, and it falls in the first 20 seconds, where gravitas matters most ("Countries would fall. Cities would burn."). The 30 s window is misleading: `lead` 1.6 s and `mustEndBy` 22.8 s leave only 21.2 s for speech.

- **Hook strength:** Moderate. It opens on a date and place, puts the stakes inside 10 s and ends on the title's questions, so it meets the checklist minimum. It is generic, though: no people, no "decisions" angle, and present tense mixed with "would" (#2). **Recommendation: yes, run the hook pass before the user signs off checkpoint 1.** That way the user approves the final script once instead of twice. However, the pass must be **word-neutral or shorter (≤ ~50 words, speed ≤ ~1.10×)**. The specialist's ~60-word illustrative rewrite would need roughly 1.27× to fit, which is over the 1.17× cap (#4). Within that budget, the best suggestions to take are #3 (the title's own questions, which also saves words), #6 (consistent tense), #5 (separate Europe from the world) and, if it fits, #2 (the "decisions, made by people" line). Any new hook line that states a fact needs a quick check by the researcher. This is a recommendation only. It is the user's decision, because the user scoped the hook out of this pass.
