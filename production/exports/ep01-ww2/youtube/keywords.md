## British vs American naming

- **"Second World War"** is the traditional British form (The Times used it from 3 September 1939; UK institutions such as IWM, The National Archives and BBC Bitesize use it). **"World War II" / "WW2"** became the US standard (Truman approved "World War II" for government use in September 1945) and is widely used internationally and in casual UK speech/search.
- Comparable explainer videos and summary sites found in research overwhelmingly use "World War 2", "WW2" or "World War II" in titles; UK education/revision pages (BBC Bitesize, Save My Exams, AQA revision notes) use "Second World War" or "World War Two".
- **Recommendation: use both.** Title uses "World War II" (the agreed working title and the widest-reach form). Narration, captions and on-screen text stay British ("the Second World War", as the script already does — this is consistent with CLAUDE.md). The description's first two lines should include "the Second World War" so both forms appear above the fold. Tags include World War II, World War 2, WW2, WWII and Second World War.
- Other spellings in metadata: British English throughout (e.g. "organised", "defence", "programme", "centre"). Dates as "1 September 1939" in the description. "D-Day" hyphenated. "VE Day" / "VJ Day" without full stops.

## Search-intent notes

- **Question intent dominates.** Results for "how did World War 2 start and end" are mostly Q&A-style pages ("When Did World War II Start and End?", "How and when did WW2 end?", "World War II: your questions answered"). Our title should answer the question pair directly — it does.
- **Causes searchers want multi-causal answers.** Study-guide results ("Causes of World War II", BBC Bitesize "long and short term factors", AP/GCSE revision) frame causes as long-term (Versailles, Depression, failure of collective security, expansionism) versus the trigger (Poland, 1 Sept 1939). Our 00:30–02:30 chapters match this — name chapters so revision-minded viewers can jump to them.
- **End searchers hit the Japan debate.** "Why did Japan surrender" results (History.co.uk, National Archives blog, Wikipedia, National WWII Museum) present the bombs vs Soviet entry debate. The script's closing line on the debate is a strength; a chapter title like "How the war ended (Germany and Japan)" signals that we cover both surrenders.
- **Format intent: short overviews.** Many results are "WW2 in 5 minutes", "in 10 minutes", Crash Course, OverSimplified (two-part, humorous), Armchair Historian (long animated). Our niche: a single 10-minute, serious-but-accessible animated overview. "10 minutes" can go in the description; optional in the title (see title-options.md).
- **Education intent.** UK revision sites (GCSE/IGCSE) and US AP World History reviews rank for causes queries. Description could note "useful as an introduction or revision overview" — without claiming curriculum alignment we haven't verified.
- **Sensitive-topic searchers** (Holocaust, Hiroshima) should find a sombre, non-graphic treatment; include a short content note in the description. Do not use these topics as thumbnail bait.
