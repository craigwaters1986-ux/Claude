# Hook review (00:00–00:30) — ep01-ww2

_youtube-specialist, 2026-11-15. Reviewed: draft v1 of `production/scripts/ep01-ww2/script.md`, section "The Hook" (50 words). For the scriptwriter via the Director; the script has not been edited. Cross-checked with the researcher's `production/research/ep01-ww2/fact-check-script-part1.md` (claims 1–4). I make no claims about retention figures; points below are craft judgements._

## Current hook

> Summer, 1939. Across Europe, life goes on as normal. But within weeks, the world would be plunged into the deadliest war in human history. Countries would fall. Cities would burn. Tens of millions of people would die. So how did the world get here? And how did it finally end?

## What works
- Opens on a date and place straight away. There's no channel intro or "in this video" preamble.
- "Countries would fall. Cities would burn." Short, rhythmic, and easy to animate.
- It finishes on the two questions the title promises, so the viewer knows what they will get.
- The researcher has checked it: "deadliest war" and "tens of millions" are VERIFIED (estimate). Nothing in it is wrong.

## Issues
1. **The first line is generic.** "Life goes on as normal" doesn't show anyone. The channel's line is "History is made by people", yet no person appears in the first 30 seconds.
2. **"As normal" overstates things.** The researcher flagged it as NUANCE. By summer 1939 the Czech lands were occupied, Danzig was in crisis and Jewish Germans were being persecuted. The suggested fix is "Across much of Europe, life seems to go on as normal".
3. **The episode's tagline is missing.** "The decisions that changed the world forever" is what sets this episode apart from other WW2 overviews, but the hook never hints that the war came from choices.
4. **"How did the world get here?" is vague.** Echoing the title ("how did it start") would be more direct and repeat the search phrase.
5. **The pace is slow for a hook.** 50 words in 30 s is about 100 wpm, against about 117 wpm for the whole script. That leaves room for 5–10 more words, or for deliberate pauses that are timed to visuals.
6. **There is no visual instruction for the first 3–5 seconds.** That is the storyboard's job, but the narration should give it a concrete image to show.
7. **It frames the war from Europe only.** That's fine for an opening image. But "the world would be plunged" sits awkwardly next to China, which had been at war since 1937. The script deals with this at 02:30, so a small wording change ("Europe will be at war; soon, the whole world") avoids the problem.

## Suggestions (in priority order)
1. **Open on people in a specific moment.** For example: "Late August, 1939. In Warsaw, London and Berlin, people go to work…". Ordinary illustrative stick figures (not presented as real individuals) give the storyboard a concrete first shot.
2. **Bring in the tagline idea in one line.** For example: "It was shaped by decisions, made by people." This sets up the episode's angle and the channel's brand together.
3. **Use the title's own questions.** End on "So how did it start? And how did it end?" instead of "How did the world get here?"
4. **Apply the researcher's nuance fix.** Use "much of Europe" and "seems to". Or switch to the people-led opening, which avoids the "normal" claim altogether.
5. **Separate Europe from the world.** "Within days, Europe will be at war: a war that will spread across the globe." This is accurate (invasion on 1 Sept, declarations on 3 Sept) and keeps Asia's earlier war for 02:30.
6. **Keep the tense consistent.** The draft moves from present ("goes on") to "would". Present-future ("will") feels more immediate in a cold open.
7. **Give the first second motion.** Storyboard note: open on movement (a tram, people walking, a newspaper seller), not a still title card. Bring the title and the channel brand in after the narration's questions, not before.
8. **Keep the sombre restraint.** Don't add explosions or comic SFX to "cities would burn". A low drone, then silence before the questions, works better.

## Illustrative rewrite (for the scriptwriter to adapt; about 60 words, about 30 s at the script's ~117 wpm)

> Late August, 1939. In Warsaw, London and Berlin, people go to work, queue at the shops and make plans for the autumn. Within days, Europe will be at war: a war that will spread across the globe and kill tens of millions. It was shaped by decisions, made by people. So how did it start? And how did it end?

Fact notes for the researcher and scriptwriter: "within days" from late August is consistent with 1 and 3 September 1939. "Tens of millions" is already VERIFIED as an estimate. "Shaped by decisions" is framing, not a single-cause claim; it stays consistent with the multi-causal 00:30 section. If anything is changed, the narration timing and SRT must be regenerated, and chapter timestamps must come from the final edit.
